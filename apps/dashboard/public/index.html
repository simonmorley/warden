Warden

Phishing triage where the AI has to earn the right to act.

Live system —

An AI can spot a phishing page in a second.

Should it be allowed to take that page down by itself?

Warden's answer: not until it has proved it, and only until one of its blocks turns out to be wrong.

  1. Recommends only Every verdict goes to a person.
  2. 73 confirmed correct
  3. On trial Cleared the bar, but still can't act. Ten clean checks to go.
  4. 10 more in a row
  5. Acts alone Blocks a URL itself. Three at most await review.

One wrong block and it falls all the way back. The block is undone and counting starts from zero.

If the evidence says it can never clear the bar, Warden says so instead of waiting forever. A different model or prompt starts a new record.

Nothing is trained

The model and prompt are fixed. Warden measures a classifier and decides what it may do; nothing it gets right or wrong feeds back in.

A verdict can't authorise itself

Whether a verdict acts is decided by arithmetic the model can't reach. A page that fools the classifier still doesn't get a block.

73 is not arbitrary

Three right out of three is 100% and proves nothing. 73 is where the evidence starts to mean something.

How to use this

  1. Score a configuration Start here. A set of pages with known answers, run through the live model: does it clear the bar, not yet, or never? Two to three minutes.
  2. Send a page Try one yourself and watch each step.
  3. Review Say whether a verdict was right. Only this moves the record.
