Warden

Phishing triage where the AI has to earn the right to act.

Live system —

An AI can spot a phishing page in a second.

Should it be allowed to take that page down by itself?

Warden's answer: not until it has proved it — and only until one of its blocks turns out to be wrong.

  1. Recommends only SHADOW Every verdict goes to a person.
  2. 73 confirmed correct
  3. On trial EARNING Strong enough — but not yet proved.
  4. 10 more in a row
  5. Acts alone AUTONOMOUS Blocks a URL itself. Three at most await review.

One wrong block and it falls all the way back — block undone, counting starts from zero.

And when the evidence says it never will — the best its accuracy could plausibly be is under the bar — it says so: Can't qualify UNQUALIFIABLE. Waiting won't help. Changing the model or the prompt will, and starts a new record from zero.

Nothing is trained

The model and prompt never change. Warden measures a fixed classifier and decides what it may do. Scoring one measures it; nothing it gets right or wrong feeds back into it.

A verdict can't authorise itself

Whether a verdict acts is decided by arithmetic the model cannot reach. A page that fools the classifier still doesn't get a block.

73 is not arbitrary

Three right out of three is 100% and means nothing. 73 is where being certain enough actually starts.

How to use this

  1. Score a configuration Start here. A set of pages whose real answers are already known, run through the live model: does this model, prompt and policy clear the bar, not yet, or never? Then see what that accuracy would do to permission on live traffic — earned, used, and lost to a page that tricks it. Two to three minutes; every verdict is a live model call.
  2. Send a page Try one yourself. Pick a test page or paste your own, and see each step: what was extracted, what the model said, whether its evidence held up, and what it was allowed to do.
  3. Review Say whether each verdict was right. This is the only thing that moves Warden's record — and only a verdict of phishing counts, because being right that a page is harmless is easy.
